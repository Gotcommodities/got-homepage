Why Zinc Matters

Zinc is the unsung protector of global infrastructure. Approximately 60% of all zinc produced is used for galvanizing — coating steel with a zinc layer to prevent corrosion. Every highway guardrail, transmission tower, bridge beam, and building frame that uses galvanized steel depends on zinc. In a world spending trillions on infrastructure renewal — the US Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, India's national highways programme, and China's Belt and Road — zinc demand follows construction activity closely.

China dominates both sides of the zinc market, producing approximately 33% of global mine output and consuming roughly 50% of refined zinc. Australia (led by Glencore and South32), Peru, India, and the United States round out the top producers. The zinc market has experienced persistent tightness in mine supply, with declining ore grades at legacy mines and limited new project development pushing treatment charges (TCs) — the fees smelters charge to process concentrate — to their lowest levels in years.

Beyond galvanizing, zinc is finding new applications in zinc-air and zinc-bromine batteries for grid-scale energy storage. These are early-stage technologies but could add meaningful demand if commercialized at scale. The zinc market is closely tied to the global industrial cycle — it rallies with infrastructure spending and weakens during recessions. With governments worldwide committing to infrastructure programmes, the medium-term demand outlook remains constructive.
