Why Uranium Matters

Uranium is in the early stages of a structural bull market driven by the global nuclear renaissance. After a decade of depressed prices following the 2011 Fukushima disaster, uranium has surged from $20/lb in 2020 to over $80/lb as governments worldwide recognize nuclear power as essential for meeting net-zero carbon targets. Over 60 new reactors are under construction globally, with China alone planning to build 150 reactors by 2035.

Kazakhstan dominates global mine production through Kazatomprom, producing approximately 43% of the world's uranium via in-situ leaching. Canada (Cameco's Athabasca Basin mines) and Australia (Olympic Dam, Ranger) round out the top three. The market faces a persistent primary supply deficit of roughly 20,000 tonnes per year, currently filled by drawdowns of commercial inventories, government stockpiles, and enrichment underfeeding — all finite secondary sources.

The emergence of small modular reactors (SMRs) adds a new demand vector. NuScale, X-energy, TerraPower, and Rolls-Royce are advancing designs that could see first commercial deployments by the late 2020s. Meanwhile, tech giants including Microsoft, Google, and Amazon have signed nuclear power purchase agreements for data centres, validating long-term uranium demand. The Sprott Physical Uranium Trust and other financial vehicles have added a speculative demand layer, removing physical pounds from the spot market.
