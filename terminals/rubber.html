Why Rubber Matters

Natural rubber is one of the few globally critical commodities that cannot be synthetically replaced at scale. Synthetic rubber (derived from petroleum) handles about 60% of total rubber demand, but aircraft tyres, heavy-duty truck tyres, surgical gloves, and high-performance applications require the superior elasticity and heat resistance of natural rubber from Hevea brasiliensis trees. The global tyre industry alone consumes roughly 70% of all NR production.

The supply side is uniquely fragile. Rubber trees take 7 years to mature before tapping can begin, creating long supply response lags. Over 85% of global NR production comes from a narrow band of Southeast Asia — Thailand, Indonesia, and Vietnam — where plantations face compounding threats: aging tree stock planted during the 1990s boom, a chronic labour shortage as smallholder farmers exit to higher-paying work, and the spread of Pestalotiopsis leaf blight, a fungal disease that has devastated plantations across Indonesia and is moving northwest.

Demand is structurally growing. China consumes over 40% of global NR for its tyre and auto manufacturing sectors. India's rapidly expanding vehicle fleet is the second growth driver. Even the EV transition is rubber-positive: EVs are heavier than ICE vehicles, wearing tyres faster and requiring premium compounds. The International Rubber Study Group (IRSG) projects a structural NR supply deficit through 2028, making rubber one of the quietest bull stories in commodities.
