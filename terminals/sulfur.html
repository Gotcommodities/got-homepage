03 — WHY IT MATTERS

The Most Interconnected Commodity Nobody Covers

Sulfur is a byproduct of oil and gas refining — and the essential input for sulfuric acid, the world's most produced industrial chemical. Sulfuric acid is required for copper leaching (hydrometallurgy), phosphate fertilizer production, oil refining alkylation, and dozens of other industrial processes. When sulfur supply tightens, the effects cascade across seemingly unrelated industries.

China imported 56% of its sulfur from the Middle East before the current conflict. Hormuz disruption is directly squeezing sulfuric acid supply globally, with CFR Southeast Asia prices elevated at $1,100–1,300 per tonne. The Middle East FOB benchmarks (Kuwait $765, Qatar $740) reflect the difficulty of getting material through the strait.

The sulfur–copper connection is particularly critical: approximately 30% of global copper production relies on sulfuric acid for leaching. Tight sulfur means tighter copper means constrained electrification. This makes sulfur a hidden bottleneck in the global energy transition.

⚠ The Sulfur Cascade

Hormuz Disruption → Middle East Sulfur Offline → Sulfuric Acid Shortage → Copper Leaching Disrupted → Phosphate Fertilizer Squeezed → Oil Refining Constrained → Electrification Bottleneck + Food Security Risk

Key Consumers

Fertilizer production (phosphoric acid) consumes approximately 50% of global sulfur. Copper mining (leaching/SX-EW) takes about 15%. Oil refining, chemicals, and metal smelting account for the rest. China is the world's largest sulfur importer.
