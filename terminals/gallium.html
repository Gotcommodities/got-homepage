03 — WHY IT MATTERS

The Invisible Chokepoint

Gallium and germanium are two of the most strategically important metals in the semiconductor supply chain — and almost nobody outside the industry tracks them. China produces 98% of the world's gallium and 60% of its germanium. In August 2023, Beijing imposed export controls on both metals, requiring case-by-case approval for every shipment. The result: a 94% collapse in Chinese gallium exports.

Gallium is essential for gallium arsenide (GaAs) and gallium nitride (GaN) semiconductors — the chips powering 5G base stations, defense radar systems, satellite communications, and LED lighting. Germanium is critical for fiber optic cables, infrared optics (night vision, thermal imaging), and solar cells. Neither has a substitute that delivers equivalent performance.

Ex-China gallium prices hit a record $1,850 per kilogram as non-Chinese supply remains negligible. China suspended its export prohibition to the US in mid-2026 with conditions — a partial, revocable thaw that keeps Western chipmakers dependent on Beijing's goodwill. Japan, South Korea, and Europe are scrambling to build alternative supply chains, but gallium recovery from alumina refining takes years to scale.

⚠ Export Control Status

China's export controls remain in effect. The partial suspension of the US prohibition comes with conditions and is revocable. 94% of Chinese gallium exports have been cut. Ex-China spot prices are at all-time highs.

04 — SUPPLY CHAIN

Global Production

Gallium Production

China~98%
Japan~1%
Others~1%

Gallium is a byproduct of alumina refining (Bayer process). China's dominance comes from being the world's largest aluminum producer. Scaling non-Chinese supply requires building gallium recovery circuits at alumina refineries worldwide — a multi-year process.

Germanium Production

China~60%
Russia~5%
Belgium (Umicore)~15%
Canada~10%
Others~10%

Germanium is recovered from zinc ore processing and coal fly ash. Belgium's Umicore is the largest non-Chinese producer. Germanium has a more diversified supply base than gallium, but China still dominates.

End-Use Applications

Gallium → GaAs semiconductors (5G, LEDs, solar cells), GaN power devices (EV chargers, defense radar, satellite comms)

Germanium → Fiber optic cables, infrared optics (night vision, thermal imaging), polymerization catalysts (PET plastics), solar cells
