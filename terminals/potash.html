03 — WHY IT MATTERS

Food Security in a Bag of Salt

Potash (potassium chloride, KCl) is one of the three essential macronutrients for plant growth — alongside nitrogen and phosphorus. Without potash, global crop yields would fall by an estimated 20–30%. There is no substitute at scale. Every country that farms needs potash, and only a handful produce it.

The potash market is dominated by a geographic oligopoly: Canada (Nutrien, formerly Potash Corp), Russia (Uralkali), and Belarus (Belaruskali) together account for roughly 60% of global production. Western sanctions on Belarus since 2021, and partial restrictions on Russian exports, have reshaped trade flows without meaningfully reducing supply — material is rerouted through non-aligned buyers.

MOP CFR Brazil — the world's most-watched potash benchmark — fell to $367.50 per tonne in September 2026, down from $396.50 in July, as the market remains well supplied. China's 2026 benchmark settled at $348 per tonne, while India's BPC contract came in at $383. The India-China spread of $35 per tonne reflects India's higher logistical costs and credit terms.

Key Importers

Brazil is the world's largest potash importer (~12 Mt/yr), followed by China, India, the United States, and Southeast Asian nations. Brazil imports approximately 95% of its potash needs.

Sanctions Impact

Western sanctions on Belarus (since 2021) and partial restrictions on Russia have not removed their supply from the market. Instead, material is routed through China, Turkey, and other non-aligned buyers, then re-exported. Net global supply has remained relatively stable.
