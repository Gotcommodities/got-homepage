Why Vanadium Matters

Vanadium is a dual-use critical mineral with an established role in high-strength steel alloys and a transformative future in grid-scale energy storage. Over 90% of current vanadium consumption goes into steel — adding as little as 0.1% vanadium to structural steel increases its tensile strength by up to 100%, reducing weight and material costs in construction, pipelines, and automotive frames.

The long-term bull case rests on vanadium redox flow batteries (VRFBs). Unlike lithium-ion, VRFBs can discharge for 4–12 hours without degradation, last 25+ years with minimal capacity loss, and scale power and energy independently. This makes them ideal for grid-scale storage — balancing wind and solar intermittency. China has already commissioned 100+ MWh VRFB installations, and a single large order can move the entire vanadium market given its relatively small size (~110,000 tonnes per year).

China dominates with approximately 75% of global mine production (~82,000 tonnes in 2024), followed by Russia and South Africa. A 50% tariff on Brazilian vanadium products took effect in August 2025 under US trade policy, potentially disrupting the supply chain for American steelmakers and VRFB manufacturers who depend on imported V₂O₅.
