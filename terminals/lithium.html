Why Lithium Matters

Lithium is the foundational metal of the electric vehicle revolution. Every EV battery — whether NMC, LFP, or solid-state — requires lithium as its core cathode and electrolyte material. Global EV sales surpassed 17 million units in 2024, and battery demand for grid-scale energy storage is growing even faster. The long-term demand trajectory is structurally bullish: the IEA projects lithium demand will grow 4–6x by 2040 under net-zero scenarios.

The short-term reality, however, is severe oversupply. A massive wave of mine and refinery expansion — triggered by the 2022 price spike to ¥600,000/t (over $80,000) — has flooded the market with excess capacity. Chinese lithium carbonate has crashed over 80% from its peak, forcing Australian spodumene miners to cut production as spot prices fall below $1,000/t (their breakeven). The Guangzhou Futures Exchange (GFEX), which launched lithium carbonate contracts in 2023, has amplified price discovery and volatility.

The "lithium triangle" of Argentina, Bolivia, and Chile holds over half the world's identified lithium resources in brine deposits, while hard-rock spodumene mining in Australia provides the marginal supply. China dominates refining — processing over 65% of global lithium chemicals regardless of where the ore is mined. The market likely needs 12–18 months to absorb excess capacity before a sustainable price recovery, but the entry of sovereign wealth funds and strategic stockpiling programs (US, EU, India, Japan) underscores the metal's long-term critical status.
