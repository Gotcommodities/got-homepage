Why Tungsten Matters

Tungsten has the highest melting point of any metal (3,422°C), the highest tensile strength, and exceptional hardness — properties that make it irreplaceable in cutting tools, drilling equipment, armor-piercing ammunition, and high-temperature applications. Tungsten carbide, the primary commercial product, is second only to diamond in hardness and is used in approximately 60% of all metal-cutting tools worldwide.

China controls approximately 82% of global tungsten production and an even higher share of processing. Beijing has classified tungsten as a strategic mineral and imposed export quotas and licensing requirements, restricting access for Western manufacturers. Vietnam, Russia, Bolivia, and Rwanda produce small amounts, but non-Chinese supply is insufficient to meet demand if China further restricts exports.

The defense dimension makes tungsten acutely geopolitical. Armor-piercing tank rounds, kinetic energy penetrators, and military-grade tooling all require tungsten. The US and EU have designated it a critical mineral, and the Pentagon has funded stockpiling and alternative supply development. However, establishing new mines and processing facilities outside China takes 7-10 years. Meanwhile, tungsten demand grows steadily with industrial activity, aerospace manufacturing, and semiconductor equipment production — every chip fabrication tool uses tungsten components. The supply concentration risk is comparable to rare earths.
