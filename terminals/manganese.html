Why Manganese Matters

Manganese is the forgotten critical metal — the fourth most used metal in the world by volume, present in virtually all steel (roughly 90% of manganese goes into steelmaking), yet rarely discussed in commodity markets. Every tonne of steel requires approximately 7 kg of manganese as a deoxidizer and alloying agent. Without it, steel becomes brittle and unworkable. There is no substitute at scale.

South Africa holds approximately 80% of the world's manganese reserves in the Kalahari Manganese Field, the single largest known deposit. Gabon (Eramet's Moanda mine), Australia, and Ghana round out the top producers. China is the dominant consumer and processor, converting ore into electrolytic manganese metal (EMM), manganese sulfate, and ferroalloys. The supply chain is long and fragmented — ore is mined in Africa, shipped to China, processed, and re-exported globally.

The emerging demand story is EV batteries. Manganese-rich cathode chemistries — including LMFP (lithium manganese iron phosphate) and high-manganese NMC — are gaining traction as automakers seek to reduce dependence on cobalt and nickel. Tesla, CATL, and BYD have all invested in manganese-rich battery development. This could add 1-2 million tonnes of annual demand by 2035, on top of the 20+ million tonnes consumed by steelmakers. The battery-grade manganese supply chain barely exists outside China, creating a future bottleneck.
