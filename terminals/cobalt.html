Why Cobalt Matters

Cobalt is at a structural crossroads. For a decade it was the indispensable cathode material in lithium-ion batteries — the NMC (nickel-manganese-cobalt) chemistry that powered the first wave of electric vehicles. But the rapid adoption of lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cathodes, which use zero cobalt, is fundamentally reshaping demand. Tesla, BYD, and most Chinese EV makers have shifted to LFP for standard-range vehicles, and LFP now accounts for over 40% of global EV battery capacity.

The Democratic Republic of Congo controls approximately 70% of global mine production, making cobalt one of the most geographically concentrated commodities on Earth. In response to collapsing prices (cobalt fell from over $80,000/t in 2022 to under $30,000 in 2024), the DRC imposed export quotas of 96,600 tonnes per year through state mining company Gécamines, aiming to stabilize prices the way OPEC manages oil output.

Despite the LFP headwind, cobalt retains critical demand from aerospace superalloys (jet engine turbine blades), high-performance NMC batteries for premium EVs and grid storage, and industrial catalysts. The ethical dimension — artisanal mining in the DRC involves documented child labor and hazardous conditions — continues to drive Western OEMs toward cobalt-free chemistry or responsibly sourced supply chains, adding structural downward pressure on demand growth.
