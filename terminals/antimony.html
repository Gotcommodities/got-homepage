03 — WHY IT MATTERS

The Pentagon's Quiet Dependency

Antimony is a military-critical mineral with no viable substitute in its two largest applications: flame retardants (~55% of demand) and lead-antimony alloys for ammunition. Every bullet casing, every fire-resistant circuit board, every military vehicle's interior lining depends on antimony trioxide. The US has near-zero domestic production and minimal stockpiles.

China controls approximately 80% of global antimony mine production and imposed civilian export restrictions in late 2024. The ban's suspension — extended through November 2026 with conditions — keeps supply flowing but leaves Western defense supply chains dependent on Beijing's tolerance. China has signaled a planned 5% production increase in H2 2026, which partially explains the recent 12.4% weekly price correction.

The US Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) signed a $245 million stockpile contract for 3,026 tonnes over five years — an acknowledgment that the current supply structure is a national security vulnerability. But 3,026 tonnes barely covers a year of US consumption.

⚠ China Export Ban Status

China's civilian antimony export prohibition is suspended through Nov 2026 with conditions. Re-imposition would immediately cut ~80% of global supply to Western buyers. The DLA stockpile covers roughly one year of US demand.

Demand Breakdown

Flame retardants: ~55% — Antimony trioxide (ATO) is the synergist in halogenated flame retardant systems. Used in electronics, textiles, automotive, construction. No cost-effective substitute at scale.

Lead-antimony alloys: ~20% — Ammunition, lead-acid batteries, cable sheathing. Antimony hardens lead — critical for ballistic performance.

Other: ~25% — Catalysts (PET plastic production), semiconductors, glass decolorizers, ceramics.

US Vulnerability

The United States produces virtually no antimony domestically. US Antimony Corp (UAMY) operates a small smelter in Montana, but relies partly on imported feedstock. The US is 84% import-dependent for antimony, with China as the primary source.
